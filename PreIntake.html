Pharmacotherapy Ideas

Patient-Specific Medication Options for Provider Review

Pharmacotherapy option 1 — optimize or continue the current regimen

Clinical rationale: Generate a patient-specific option based on the documented diagnosis, target symptoms, current medications, prior response, adverse effects, adherence, medical history, substance use, and patient goals. Explain why continuation, dose optimization, timing adjustment, formulation change, or no medication change may be reasonable. Do not prescribe or imply a finalized decision.

Before considering: List the history, vitals, labs, interaction review, pregnancy/reproductive considerations, misuse/diversion assessment, diagnostic clarification, or collateral information that must be confirmed first.

Monitoring: List patient-specific efficacy, adverse-effect, safety, adherence, and measurement-based monitoring needs, including expected reassessment timing when clinically appropriate.

Pharmacotherapy option 2 — alternative, switch, or adjunctive strategy

Clinical rationale: Generate one evidence-informed alternative or adjunctive medication class or strategy that could be discussed if supported by the available information. Tie it to specific target symptoms and prior-treatment limitations. Avoid unsupported specificity and clearly identify diagnostic uncertainty.

Risks / cautions: Identify patient-specific contraindications, interaction risks, activation or mood-switch concerns, cardiovascular or metabolic concerns, sedation, cognitive effects, sexual or reproductive effects, withdrawal/discontinuation issues, and substance-related considerations that require provider review.

Monitoring: Specify appropriate baseline and follow-up monitoring without inventing results.

Pharmacotherapy option 3 — simplification, taper, deprescribing, or defer medication change

Clinical rationale: Assess whether medication simplification, cautious taper discussion, correction of adherence or timing problems, treatment of a medical or sleep contributor, psychotherapy-first care, substance reduction, or deferring medication changes until diagnostic clarification may be clinically preferable. Include only options supported by the record.

Safeguards: Identify withdrawal risks, relapse risks, tapering cautions, coordination needs with the existing prescriber, and circumstances in which medication changes should not occur at the initial visit.
