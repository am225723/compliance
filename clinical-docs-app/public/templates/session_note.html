1. Data (Subjective & Objective)

Subjective Report

Presenting Concerns

Generate a transcript-grounded summary of the patient's stated reason for visit and primary concerns. Include direct quote only if present.

Interval History

Summarize clinically relevant changes since last contact, including symptom course, stressors, functioning, and treatment changes.

Mood and Emotion

Describe reported mood, emotional tone, affective shifts, irritability, sadness, anxiety, guilt, shame, grief, or emotional lability when documented.

Symptom Domains

Summarize documented anxiety, depression, ADHD/executive functioning, trauma, obsessive-compulsive, mood, psychotic, eating/body image, or other symptom domains relevant to the session.

Somatic / Physiological Symptoms

Document body-based symptoms, panic physiology, tension, pain, appetite changes, fatigue, sleep-related physiology, medication effects, or medical contributors discussed.

Sleep / Appetite / Energy / Concentration

Document sleep architecture, appetite, energy, focus, motivation, task initiation/completion, and neurovegetative symptoms when discussed.

Psychosocial / Occupational / Family Stressors

Summarize work, relationship, parenting, family, financial, legal, housing, identity, grief, or social stressors discussed.

Medication Adherence / Side Effects

Document current medication adherence, benefit, side effects, access/pharmacy issues, patient concerns, or absence of medication discussion.

Coping, Strengths, and Patient Goals

Extract coping strategies, insight, strengths, values, readiness for change, stated therapy goals, and desired outcomes.

Objective Observations (MSE)

Appearance and Behavior

Generate objective observations of appearance, grooming, posture, engagement, eye contact, cooperation, and telehealth limitations when relevant.

Speech and Language

Describe speech rate, rhythm, volume, articulation, fluency, spontaneity, coherence, and language organization.

Mood and Affect

Document stated mood if available and objective affect range, congruence, reactivity, stability, and intensity.

Thought Process and Content

Describe thought process and content including linearity, goal-direction, rumination, obsessions, delusions, suicidal/homicidal content, or absence of abnormalities.

Perception

Document hallucinations, illusions, dissociation, depersonalization/derealization, or no perceptual disturbance when assessed.

Cognition, Insight, and Judgment

Document alertness, orientation, attention, memory when observable, insight, judgment, impulse control, and reliability as historian.

Telehealth / Observation Limits

State any limitations of the encounter format that affected clinical observation, only when relevant.

2. Assessment

Clinical Impression

Symptom Severity and Trajectory

Assess current severity, progression or improvement, acute/chronic factors, and comparison to prior presentation when available.

Diagnostic Formulation

Connect symptoms to DSM-5-TR/ICD-10 diagnoses or diagnostic hypotheses without overclaiming unsupported data.

Psychosocial Context

Explain how stressors, relationships, occupation, identity, medical issues, trauma history, or environment shape the presentation.

Functional Impairment

Describe impairment across work/school, relationships, parenting/family role, ADLs, sleep, self-care, and emotional regulation.

Treatment Progress / Engagement

Evaluate engagement, insight, motivation, response to prior interventions, barriers, and progress toward goals.

Medical Necessity

Write a concise medically necessary rationale linking symptoms, risk, impairment, diagnosis, and need for continued treatment.

Risk Assessment

Suicidal and Homicidal Ideation

Document SI/HI presence or absence, intent, plan, means, preparatory behavior, and timeframe if assessed.

Self-Harm / NSSI

Document nonsuicidal self-injury, urges, behavior, history, or absence when assessed.

Mania, Psychosis, and Intoxication

Document symptoms or absence of mania/hypomania, psychosis, intoxication, withdrawal, or behavioral dyscontrol when assessed.

Access to Means / Environmental Risk

Document firearms, lethal medications, unsafe living situation, abuse/vulnerability, or other environmental risk factors if discussed.

Acute and Chronic Risk Level

Assign LOW, MODERATE, or HIGH acute risk and chronic risk with brief clinical justification.

Protective Factors

List protective factors such as family, children, future orientation, supports, treatment engagement, values, spirituality, work, pets, or coping skills.

Safety Planning / Contingency Plan

Document safety plan, crisis resources, emergency instructions, coping steps, support contacts, or state no formal safety plan required if low risk.

4. Plan

Medication Management

Medication Changes / Refills / Continuations

Document medication starts, refills, continuations, discontinuations, dose changes, pharmacy issues, and prescription routing. If no medication changes occurred, state that clearly.

Medication Rationale and Target Symptoms

Explain medication rationale and target symptoms when medications are started/continued/changed. If medication management did not occur, state that it did not occur and identify whether the patient refused medication, does not want medication, is ambivalent, prefers non-medication options, or whether preference was not stated.

Risks, Benefits, Side Effects, and Alternatives

Document medication counseling, side effects, patient concerns, alternatives, and informed consent when discussed. If medication was declined/refused or not wanted, state the patient preference and summarize any education provided. If no medication counseling occurred, state that clearly.

Monitoring / Labs / Vitals / Coordination

Document monitoring plan for vitals, labs, PDMP/PMP, medical comorbidities, coordination, releases, or referrals. If no medication monitoring was ordered, state any clinically relevant follow-up monitoring such as symptoms, safety, sleep, substance use, or rating scales.

No Medication Management

Always answer this field when no medication management occurred. State whether the client refused medication, does not want medication, prefers non-medication treatment, is ambivalent, or whether medication preference was not stated. Do not leave this blank.

Next Steps & Scheduling

Therapeutic Homework / Skill Practice

List specific homework, tracking, journaling, coping practice, communication task, behavioral experiment, or executive functioning tool.

Care Coordination / Referrals

Document coordination with providers, letters/forms, labs, collateral, referrals, records requests, or administrative follow-up.

Follow-Up Plan

Always provide a follow-up plan. Include recommended follow-up interval, modality, purpose of next visit, and next clinical priorities. If exact appointment date/time is not stated, leave only the exact date/time unstated while still giving the clinical follow-up plan.

Contingency / Crisis Instructions

Document what patient should do if symptoms worsen, including crisis resources or emergency care when clinically indicated.

Longer-Term Treatment Goals

Summarize ongoing treatment targets, therapeutic focus, and next clinical priorities.
